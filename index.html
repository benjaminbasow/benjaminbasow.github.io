About

Research at the intersection of health policy and economic outcomes.

I am a PhD Candidate in Economics at Colorado State University. My research uses quasi-experimental methods to study the consequences of healthcare regulation and insurance policy for hospitals, patients, and households.

My work applies modern causal inference methods including difference-in-differences, synthetic control, and related panel-data approaches to policy-relevant questions in health economics.

Selected Working Papers

01

Surprise Balance Billing Restrictions and Personal Bankruptcy

Working Paper · Health Economics · Synthetic Control

This project studies whether restrictions on surprise balance billing reduce household financial distress. Using Florida's 2016 balance-billing reform as a quasi-natural experiment, the analysis constructs a synthetic counterfactual for Florida and evaluates changes in personal bankruptcy filings.

Balance Billing Protections, Hospital Costs, and Uncompensated Care

Working Paper · Health Policy · Difference-in-Differences

This project examines how state balance-billing protections affect hospital costs, bad debt, uncompensated care, charity care, ambulance costs, operating room costs, and radiology-related expenditures.

The Affordable Care Act and Hospital Employment
